01The Concept

Paying homage to Virgil.

What was the core idea or vibe you wanted to capture with that heavy cross-stitch pattern?

The core idea for this project was my take on legendary streetwear designer Virgil Abloh's collaboration with Jordan: the Off-White / OG Jordan 1.

That shoe changed the game. It took a silhouette everyone already knew and made people look at it like it was brand new. That's the energy I wanted. Keep the Chicago red, white and black, then tear it down and rebuild it with textured exotic-print leather and heavy cord stitching wrapped across every panel.

02The Craft

The labor you don't see.

People see the final product, but not the labor. How many hours went into this pair?

About 30 hours of work went into this project.

What's the hardest part about working with thick cord on leather that people wouldn't realize from a picture?

Working with leather cord wasn't difficult for me, but it's very time consuming. Every single hole for the cord work gets punched by hand.

Look closer at the photos. Every X on that upper is two holes punched by hand and a length of cord pulled through and locked down. Now count them.

How physically demanding is a heavy-stitch build compared to a standard custom paint job?

The basic stitching was done on my industrial sewing machine, so no stress there. But the cord work was all done by hand, and that is very demanding on my hands.

The machine handles the seams. The hands handle the soul.

03 · The Matching Set

Python on the brim. A nod to Just Don.

A matching hat is a huge flex. How do you make the look carry from sneaker to headwear?

The hat was my take on legendary designer Don C, a.k.a. Just Don, who's known for his collaboration with Mitchell & Ness, putting python on the brim of a baseball cap.

A black Chicago cap gets a white python-print brim, the same dark cord laced around the edge, and a twisted red-and-white cord where the brim meets the crown. Same materials, same stitch language. Wear them together and they read as one piece.
